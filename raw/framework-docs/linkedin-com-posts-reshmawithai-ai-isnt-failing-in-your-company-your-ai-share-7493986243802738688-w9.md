AI isn’t failing in your company. Your AI pilots are.
I spoke with several leadership teams at the last conference who were genuinely serious about AI.
They had:
→ AI roadmaps
→ Multiple pilots
→ Vendor demos
→ Executive sponsorship
→ Millions being invested
And yet, almost every conversation ended with:
“We should explore this further.”
That’s the problem.
AI doesn’t create business value in a demo.
It creates value in production.
And the companies getting stuck aren’t lacking ambition.
They’re lacking the foundation to scale AI beyond isolated experiments.
Here are 7 warning signs your company is stuck in AI pilot mode:
1️⃣ Every AI use case starts from zero
Customer support, legal, CRM, finance i
2️⃣ Every pilot triggers a new architecture debate
If every workflow requires another round of “How should we build this?”, you don’t have an AI platform.
3️⃣ Security comes after the demo
Governance cannot be bolted onto enterprise AI later.
RBAC. Auditability. Guardrails. PII protection. Human oversight.
4️⃣ Integrations are slowing everything down
An agent that can only answer questions isn’t enough.
Real AI needs to safely work with:
Salesforce.
SQL.
ServiceNow.
CRMs.
ERPs.
5️⃣ Teams are building disconnected point solutions
One agent here.
Another automation there.
Another vendor somewhere else.
6️⃣ Humans are still doing the handoff work
If AI answers a question but someone still has to:
→ route the task
→ update the CRM
→ trigger the workflow
→ send the approval
→ involve the right person
7️⃣ ROI is measured after deployment
The question shouldn’t be:
“How much did we spend on AI?”
It should be:
“What business outcome are we improving?”
Quality.
Cost.
Speed.
Revenue.
Customer experience.
Risk reduction.
